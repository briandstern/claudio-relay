## Who I Am

Brian. COO of Digital at Clear Street, a fintech prime brokerage at 4 World Trade Center, NYC. I commute via LIRR from Port Washington, NY. I'm in my mid-30s, athletic build (CrossFit 6 days/week), and I care about looking styled without looking like I'm trying too hard.

## My Style in One Sentence

Fintech cool business casual — European-influenced, texture-driven, intentionally layered — that sits between "boring finance guy" and "fashion influencer." I want to be the best-dressed person in the room without anyone being able to pinpoint why.

## My Office Environment

Clear Street is a fintech, not Goldman Sachs. Some people wear work-appropriate casual (clean jeans, sneakers). Some wear boring business casual (button-down, dress pants, vest). Nobody wears suits and ties. I want to dress above both groups — styled and intentional — but never overdressed. Think: the guy who runs a business unit and dresses like he thinks about it just enough.

- Suits or sport coats with ties
- Dress shirts (spread collar, French cuff, etc.)

## Color Palette

**Core neutrals:** Navy, charcoal, black, white, cream/oatmeal

**Earth tones:** Olive, chocolate brown, camel/tan, burgundy/maroon

**Approach:** Dark tonal combinations with texture contrast. Never more than 3 main colors in an outfit. No bright colors, no pastels, no patterns (except subtle texture like marled knit or Donegal speckle).

---

## Key Styling Rules

1. **Three textures minimum** in every outfit — this is what separates "dressed" from "styled" (e.g., cashmere + oxford cloth + suede, or knit + wool + leather)
2. **Layers are the move** — a base layer + mid layer + outer layer is almost always better than just a shirt and pants
3. **Sweaters and knits stay untucked** — never tuck a crewneck or quarter-zip into trousers
4. **OCBDs tuck in under sweaters** — the shirt stays tucked, the sweater floats above the waistband
5. **Collar pop** — when wearing an OCBD under a crewneck or quarter-zip, the collar should be visible above the neckline
6. **Chelsea boots are the default office shoe** — they elevate everything
7. **Cuffed trouser hems break clean at the boot shaft** — no stacking, no pooling
8. **Leave jackets/zips open** — chore jackets, field jackets, and zip sweaters look best unbuttoned/unzipped
9. **No white athletic crew socks** with low-top sneakers — no-show or tonal socks only
10. **Blazers stay casual** — pair with knits, turtlenecks, or henleys underneath; never with a dress shirt
11. **Brown leather boots only on rain/snow days** — slight disconnect with black pants but survival trumps style
12. **Never repeat yesterday's exact outfit** — rotate pieces, swap colors, change the energy

## Weekend Context — Fashionable Dad, Not Workwear

On weekends I'm a dad in Port Washington — kids, errands, casual outings, maybe a restaurant. The vibe is **fashionable dad**: looks that are relaxed and clearly off-duty, but still intentional and sharp. This is NOT workwear recycled for Saturday.

**Weekend NEVER list (additions):**
- No blazers, pleated trousers, or Chelsea boots — that's an office outfit
- No "boring suburban dad": no cargo shorts, no Patagonia vest, no beat-up gym shoes, no oversized dad jeans, no New Balance 990s in grey
- No looks that read "I'm trying to dress like a 22-year-old" — keep it age-appropriate and cool, not hypebeast

**Weekend SHOULD look like:**
- Dark slim-straight or straight-leg jeans (no distressing) as the base
- Clean, interesting sneakers: cream/white leather, subtle color, retro silhouettes (Nikes, New Balance in non-dad colorways, Veja, etc.)
- Relaxed outerwear with shape: chore jacket, overshirt, field jacket, puffer vest over a knit
- Casualer tops: henleys, heavy crew-neck sweatshirts (no logos), thick knit polos, crewneck sweaters
- The goal: someone who looks great dropping their kid at soccer without looking like they're going to a meeting or a frat reunion

## Weather Awareness

| Condition | Rule |
|-----------|------|

| 40–55°F | Quarter-zip or sweater weather, light jacket |
